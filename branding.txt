==========================================================================
1. GLYPH OR CUSTOMISABLE ICONS?
==========================================================================

They're two different things, and you keep both.

THE CUSTOMISABLE ICONS (what you have)
  Classic, Usage, Timer, Ultra. These choose WHAT INFORMATION the menu bar
  shows: a status mark, a ring with your percentage, a countdown, or both.

THE GLYPH (what's missing)
  Tuloy's own little drawn symbol. It decides WHAT TULOY LOOKS LIKE.

Right now Tuloy has no symbol of its own. In Classic it borrows Apple's
stock pictures: a tick in a circle, a pause sign, a moon, a warning
triangle. Thousands of apps use the same four. Nothing in your menu bar
says "that one is Tuloy".

With a glyph:
  - Classic shows the Tuloy symbol, in four versions of the same drawing:
    running, paused, resting, needs attention.
  - Usage and Ultra keep the ring, but the ring can carry the symbol's
    shape (a small gap, an arrowhead, a notch at the top) so it's still
    recognisably Tuloy.
  - Timer stays plain numbers.

So: the modes stay exactly as they are. The glyph is the one drawing that
ties them together. It's the small, one-colour sibling of the app icon:

  App icon          big, full colour. Dock, Finder, About, the website.
  Menu bar glyph    tiny, one colour. The menu bar, and inside the ring.


==========================================================================
2. WHAT THE NAME GIVES US
==========================================================================

"Tuloy" carries two meanings in Filipino, and both fit:

  KEEP GOING / CONTINUE
    What the app does: one window hands over to the next and you carry on.
    Visual ideas: forward arrows, a loop that never closes, a relay, a
    path, a play symbol.

  COME IN / WELCOME  ("Tuloy po kayo": please come in)
    What it feels like: a fresh window opening for you.
    Visual ideas: an open door, a window with light coming through, a
    doorway, a sunrise.

Other things in the idea itself:
  - A WINDOW. Claude's "window" is a stretch of time, but the word invites
    a literal one. Capiz-shell windows, the sliding windows of old Filipino
    houses with their grid of pale shell panes, are a lovely match.
  - FIVE HOURS. A 5-part ring, or one arc that is 5/24 of a circle.
  - FRESH. Morning, dew, citrus, a clean pane of glass.
  - A PING. One small signal: a dot with a ripple, a paper plane.

Filipino sources worth borrowing from, lightly:
  - the eight-rayed sun
  - banig (woven mat) patterns
  - capiz-shell windows
  - jeepney colours and chrome
  - ube, calamansi, mango, pandan, sampaguita
  - Baybayin script. "Tuloy" is roughly ᜆᜓᜎᜓᜌ᜔. Have someone who reads
    Baybayin confirm it before it goes anywhere near a logo.

One caution: the Philippine flag itself has legal protection against use
in trade marks and commercial designs. A stylised sun is common and fine in
spirit; don't reproduce the flag or its exact arrangement.

3. WHERE THE BRAND SHOWS UP
==========================================================================

  Menu bar glyph        about 16 to 18 points, one colour. Seen all day.
  The rings             menu bar, mini window, popover, Home.
  Accent colour         buttons, the selected sidebar row, the timeline.
  App icon              Dock (when a window is open), Finder, About.
  Windows               Welcome's first page, About, empty states.
  Notifications         the icon beside every notification.
  Outside the app       README, a download page, the disk image.

The menu bar glyph is seen a hundred times more than anything else. Design
it first and make the app icon follow from it, not the other way round.


==========================================================================
4. RULES A DESIGN HAS TO OBEY
==========================================================================

THE MENU BAR GLYPH
  - One colour only. macOS draws menu bar icons in black or white to suit
    the wallpaper. No colours, no shades of grey.
  - It has to read at 16 points. That is tiny: roughly three clear shapes
    at most, lines no thinner than 1.5 points.
  - Four states that are clearly the same symbol: running, paused,
    resting, needs attention.
  - It has to sit comfortably beside Wi-Fi, battery and the clock:
    similar weight, similar height.

THE APP ICON
  - macOS shape: a rounded square, drawn at 1024 points, still clear at 32.
  - One idea, not three. If you have to explain it, it's too much.

COLOUR
  - Button text must be readable: white on the colour needs a contrast of
    4.5 to 1 or better. I've worked this out for every colour below.
  - Orange and red are already taken: they mean "nearly out" and "needs
    attention". An orange brand would make everything look like a warning.
    If the brand IS orange, the warning colour has to move to red.
  - Green is taken too, more loosely: ticks and "it works".
  - It needs a second version for dark mode. A colour that's strong on
    white is usually too dim on black.

NOT ANTHROPIC'S
  - Stay away from Anthropic's warm clay orange and its starburst mark, so
    nobody mistakes Tuloy for an official app.

A CHOICE TO MAKE EARLY
  Today Tuloy's buttons and rings follow each person's own macOS accent
  colour (blue unless they changed it). A brand colour means overriding
  that. Both are respectable:
    Follow the Mac     feels native, costs nothing, no identity.
    Own colour         recognisable, needs light and dark versions.
  A middle way: own colour for the icon and rings, the Mac's colour for
  buttons.

5. COLOUR SCHEMES
==========================================================================

For each: the main colour (buttons, rings), a partner, and a dark-mode
version. "White text" is the contrast of white on the main colour; 4.5 or
more passes, 3.0 to 4.4 is fine for large text only, under 3 fails.

 1. UBE
    Main        #6B3FA0  deep purple        white text 7.4  passes
    Dark mode   #A78BFA  soft violet
    Partner     cream #FBF7EF, or leche-flan yellow #FCD116 as a spark
    Feel        Distinctly Filipino, warm, a little playful. Nobody else in
                the menu bar is purple.
    Watch       Purple can read as "AI product" cliché. The cream keeps it
                food-warm rather than tech-cold.
    Warnings    Orange and red stay free. No clash.

 2. DAWN
    Main        #4F46E5  indigo             white text 6.3  passes
    Dark mode   #A78BFA or a lighter indigo
    Partner     a gradient to pink #EC4899 and amber #F59E0B, icon only
    Feel        A fresh window as a sunrise: night turning to morning.
                Modern, optimistic.
    Watch       Gradients belong on the app icon. Buttons stay flat indigo.
    Warnings    Free.

 3. TROPICAL SEA
    Main        #0E7C86  deep teal          white text 4.9  passes
    Dark mode   #14B8A6  bright teal
    Partner     coral #FF6B5E, or sand #F3E9D2
    Feel        Calm, clear water. "Fresh" without being a morning cliché.
    Watch       Coral sits close to the warning orange. Use it in the icon,
                not in the app.
    Warnings    Free.

 4. SAMPAGUITA
    Main        #2F6B4F  jasmine-leaf green  white text 6.3  passes
    Dark mode   #4CAF7A
    Partner     flower white #FFFDF6, a touch of gold #D4A017
    Feel        Quiet, natural, elegant. The national flower.
    Watch       Green already means "it works". A green brand makes ticks
                invisible unless they move to another colour.
    Warnings    Free, but see above.

 5. MANGO SUNRISE
    Main        #C2410C  deep mango         white text 5.2  passes
    Bright      #F59E0B  amber              white text 2.1  fails: dark
                                            text only
    Dark mode   #F59E0B with dark text
    Partner     night navy #0F1B3D
    Feel        Warm, energetic, morning. Very "fresh start".
    Watch       Two problems. It IS the warning colour, so warnings must
                become red. And it sits near Anthropic's orange. I'd keep
                mango for the icon's sunrise and not for buttons.

 6. CALAMANSI
    Main        #3F7D20  leaf green         white text 5.0  passes
    Bright      #84CC16  lime               white text 2.0  fails: dark
                                            text only
    Partner     off-white #F7F9EE
    Feel        Zesty, fresh, unmistakable. The most "fresh" of all.
    Watch       Lime is loud; a little goes a long way. Same green clash
                as Sampaguita.

 7. CAPIZ PEARL
    Main        #3A8F8A  soft teal          white text 3.8  large text
                                            only; darken to #2C726E for
                                            buttons
    Partner     pearl #F4F1EA, graphite #374151
    Feel        The capiz window: pale, luminous, gentle. Sophisticated.
    Watch       Pale palettes can look washed out at small sizes. The icon
                would need a strong graphite frame.
    Warnings    Free.

 8. MODERN FLAG
    Main        #0038A8  royal blue         white text 9.9  passes
    Dark mode   needs a lighter blue such as #5B8DEF (the royal blue is
                almost invisible on black)
    Partner     scarlet #CE1126, sun gold #FCD116
    Feel        Proudly Filipino, bold, immediately placed.
    Watch       Blue is every app's default; and red plus yellow are
                exactly the warning colours. Use red and gold only in the
                icon. And mind the flag caution in part 2.

 9. JEEPNEY
    Main        #E11D48  jeepney red        white text 4.7  passes
    Partner     chrome grey #C9CED6, with yellow and blue stripes
    Feel        Loud, joyful, street-level. "Keep going" on wheels.
    Watch       Red everywhere reads as "error". Best as an icon style with
                a calmer colour inside the app.

10. BANIG
    Main        #2E3A6B  woven indigo       white text 10.9  passes
    Dark mode   needs a lighter indigo
    Partner     straw #E3C98A, terracotta #B5532A
    Feel        Handmade, warm, patterned. The weave could be a background
                texture on the welcome page.
    Watch       Terracotta is near Anthropic's colour. Keep it to thin
                pattern lines.

11. NARRA
    Main        #8B5A2B  narra wood         white text 5.8  passes
    Partner     gold #D4A017, cream
    Feel        Warm, grounded, "old house with capiz windows".
    Watch       Brown is hard to make feel modern on a screen.

12. MANILA BAY SUNSET
    Main        #C026D3  magenta            white text 4.7  passes
    Partner     a gradient to orange and gold, icon only
    Feel        Dramatic, memorable, a bit glamorous.
    Watch       Strong personality: you'll love it or tire of it.

13. HALO-HALO
    Main        any of: ube #6B3FA0, pandan #4CAF7A, leche flan #FCD116,
                pink #EC4899
    Feel        Many colours, playful, sweet. Each state or each ring
                segment a different flavour.
    Watch       Hard to keep tidy. Works best with ube as the one "main"
                and the others as small accents: which is scheme 1 with
                sprinkles.

14. MIDNIGHT
    Main        #22D3EE  electric cyan      white text 1.8  fails: dark
                                            text only
    Background  near-black #0B1020
    Feel        For people who live in dark mode and a terminal.
    Watch       Only really works dark. Light mode needs a different main
                colour, which is two brands.

15. GRAPHITE AND ONE SIGNAL
    Main        #374151  graphite           white text 10.3  passes
    Signal      one small bright colour: ube, teal or lime
    Feel        Calm and professional. The app stays grey; the one colour
                appears only where something is alive (the ring's fill).
    Watch       Can feel plain. But it's the one that never fights the
                warnings, because almost nothing is coloured.

16. FOLLOW THE MAC
    Main        whatever accent colour the person chose in macOS
    Feel        Completely native. Zero design risk.
    Watch       No colour identity at all. The glyph and icon carry it.

6. LOGO IDEAS
==========================================================================

For each: the idea, how the tiny menu bar glyph would look, how its four
states work, and what's good and bad.

 1. THE OPEN RING
    A circle that doesn't quite close, with a small arrowhead on the open
    end pointing forward. "It keeps going."
    Glyph     the ring with its gap at top right.
    States    running: arrowhead. paused: gap, no arrowhead, two short
              bars inside. resting: a dot asleep in the gap. attention:
              the gap filled by a small "!".
    Good      it IS the usage ring, so every mode already looks like it.
    Bad       circular arrows mean "refresh" to everyone. Keep the arrow
              subtle or it reads as a reload button.

 2. THE CAPIZ WINDOW
    A square of four or nine panes, one pane bright. A fresh window,
    literally.
    Glyph     a 2 by 2 grid with one pane filled.
    States    running: one pane filled. paused: all outlined. resting: all
              filled, dimmed. attention: one pane with a "!".
    Good      unique in a menu bar; ties to "window" and to home.
    Bad       a 2 by 2 grid looks like the Windows logo. Nine panes or a
              rounded outer frame fixes that.

 3. THE OPEN DOOR
    A doorway with the door ajar and light coming through. "Tuloy po
    kayo."
    Glyph     a rectangle with a slanted inner edge.
    States    door open / door closed / door with a moon / door with "!".
    Good      the warmest, most human idea. The states are obvious.
    Bad       says "welcome" more than "usage". Could be mistaken for
              "log out", which often uses a door.

 4. THE RELAY
    Two arcs, one ending as the next begins, slightly overlapped: one
    window handing to the next.
    Glyph     two quarter-circle strokes chasing each other.
    States    both solid / one solid one faint / both faint / broken.
    Good      shows exactly what keep going does.
    Bad       abstract. Needs the name beside it to mean anything.

 5. THE SUNRISE
    A half sun on a horizon with rays (eight, quietly). A fresh window as
    a new morning.
    Glyph     a semicircle on a line with three or five rays.
    States    sun up / sun half below / moon / sun with "!".
    Good      instantly "fresh start"; nods to the Philippine sun.
    Bad       sunrise icons are common (weather, alarms). Fine rays vanish
              at 16 points, so few and thick.

 6. THE FORWARD T
    The letter T, its crossbar ending in an arrow or its stem leaning
    forward as if walking.
    Glyph     a T with a chevron on the right of the bar.
    States    the chevron changes: > running, || paused, z resting, !
              attention.
    Good      a letter mark is simple and ownable.
    Bad       tells you nothing about what it does.

 7. THE DOUBLE CHEVRON
    Two forward chevrons, like fast-forward, inside a ring.
    Glyph     >> in a circle.
    States    >> / || / zz / !
    Good      "keep going" in the plainest possible way. The four states
              are a perfect set.
    Bad       looks like a media button.

 8. THE PING
    A dot with one or two ripples going outward.
    Glyph     a dot and two arcs.
    States    rippling / still dot / dim dot / dot with "!".
    Good      shows the action; friendly.
    Bad       looks like Wi-Fi, AirDrop or a location pulse.

 9. THE FIVE-HOUR ARC
    A clock face where one bold arc covers five hours, with a notch where
    the next begins.
    Glyph     a circle with one thick arc, about a fifth of the way round.
    States    the arc moves round as the window passes: a live icon.
    Good      informative; the glyph and the usage ring become one thing.
    Bad       at 16 points it's just "a ring with a thick bit".

10. THE PAPER PLANE
    A small paper plane on a looping path.
    Glyph     the plane alone.
    States    flying / landed / asleep / crossed.
    Good      it's already the symbol on the Ping Now button.
    Bad       it's every messaging app's send button.

11. THE WEAVE
    Two strands crossing over and under, from banig weaving: one day woven
    into the next.
    Glyph     a simple over-under knot of two lines.
    States    harder: tight / loose / unravelled.
    Good      beautiful as an app icon and as a pattern.
    Bad       too intricate for a menu bar. App icon only, with a simpler
              glyph beside it.

12. BAYBAYIN
    The first syllable, "tu", as a mark in a circle or a rounded square.
    Glyph     the single character.
    States    a dot or bar added around it.
    Good      meaningful, striking, uniquely Filipino.
    Bad       most people can't read it, and it must be written correctly.
              Check with a reader first.

13. THE HOURGLASS THAT TURNS ITSELF
    An hourglass tilted mid-flip, with a small arrow: time that refills.
    Glyph     a tilted hourglass.
    States    tilted / upright / on its side / cracked.
    Good      "time that resets" is exactly right.
    Bad       hourglass means "wait" to most people. Slightly negative.

14. THE ENSO
    One brush-stroke circle with a gap. Calm, hand-made, unfinished on
    purpose.
    Glyph     a thick uneven ring.
    States    weight and gap change.
    Good      beautiful; sits naturally around the usage number.
    Bad       brush texture disappears in one colour at 16 points, leaving
              a plain ring: idea 1 without the arrow.

15. THE STEPPING STONES
    Three or four dots in a rising line, the last one bright: one window
    after another.
    Glyph     three dots stepping up to the right.
    States    all lit / none / dim / last one "!".
    Good      simple, cheerful, forward-moving.
    Bad       close to "loading" or signal bars.

16. THE WINDOW AND THE SUN (a combination)
    A capiz window frame with a sun rising in it: ideas 2 and 5 together,
    for the app icon. The glyph is just the frame with one pane lit.
    Good      tells the whole story at Dock size.
    Bad       two ideas in one icon; has to be drawn with great restraint.

7. PAIRINGS THAT WORK
==========================================================================

  The Open Ring + Ube
      A purple ring with a small gap on cream. Simple, modern, yours.

  The Capiz Window + Capiz Pearl or Dawn
      Pale shell panes with one glowing pane; or the panes tinted like a
      sunrise. The most distinctive and the most Filipino.

  The Open Door + Mango Sunrise (icon only) with Tropical Sea inside the
  app
      Warm light through a doorway on the icon; calm teal for buttons, so
      nothing in the app looks like a warning.

  The Sunrise + Dawn
      A sun coming up through an indigo-to-amber sky.

  The Double Chevron + Graphite and one signal
      Quiet grey app, one bright >> where it matters. The most
      "professional tool" of the lot.

  The Relay + Tropical Sea
      Two teal arcs. Clean and abstract.

8. TYPE AND VOICE
==========================================================================

TYPE
  Inside the app, keep Apple's system font: it's what makes a Mac app feel
  like a Mac app, and it costs nothing. Use the rounded version for
  numbers in the rings (already done): it's friendlier.

  For the name "Tuloy" on the icon, the website and About, one of:
    - the system font, rounded, heavy. Safe, matches the app.
    - a geometric sans with a round "o", so the o can BE the ring.
    - a warm humanist sans, for the door or window directions.
  The "o" in Tuloy sits right in the middle of the word. Making it the
  ring or the window is an obvious, good move.

TAGLINE IDEAS
  Keep going.
  Fresh windows, right on time.
  Your next window, already waiting.
  Start early. Keep going.
  Tuloy lang.            ("just keep going": casual, very Filipino)
  Tuloy po kayo.         (for the welcome window: "come in")

VOICE
  The app already has one: plain, warm, short sentences, no jargon. Keep
  it. Brand it by being the menu bar app that explains itself.

9. HOW TO CHOOSE WITHOUT ARGUING
==========================================================================

Run every candidate through the same six checks. Most fail the first.

  1. THE 16-POINT TEST
     Shrink the glyph to menu bar size, in black, and put it in a
     screenshot of your real menu bar between two other icons. Can you
     tell what it is? Can you tell the four states apart?

  2. THE SQUINT TEST
     Squint at the app icon at Dock size. Is there one clear shape?

  3. BLACK AND WHITE
     Does the logo still work with no colour at all? The glyph has to.

  4. LIGHT AND DARK
     Try the colour on a white window and a black one. Demo mode can show
     every screen in both.

  5. COLOUR-BLIND CHECK
     View the screens through a colour-blindness simulator. Usage, warning
     and "it works" must still be told apart, by shape if not by colour.

  6. FIVE PEOPLE
     Show three finalists to five people for five seconds each. Ask "what
     does this app do?" and "which would you click?" Count, don't debate.

A seventh, for you alone: live with the glyph in your own menu bar for
three days. The one you stop noticing in a good way wins.


==========================================================================
10. MY SHORTLIST
==========================================================================

If you asked me to narrow it to three directions to sketch:

  A. THE OPEN RING, in UBE
     Why: the ring is already the heart of the app, so the brand and the
     product become one shape. Purple is free of every warning colour and
     has a real Filipino story. Lowest risk, easiest to draw, works at 16
     points.

  B. THE CAPIZ WINDOW, in DAWN or CAPIZ PEARL
     Why: the most original and the most meaningful. A window, for an app
     about windows, from Filipino houses. Harder to get right small; worth
     a sketch to find out.

  C. THE OPEN DOOR on a sunrise icon, with TROPICAL SEA inside the app
     Why: it leads with the second meaning, "come in", which is the warm
     one. Best welcome screen of the three.

What I'd avoid as the main colour: orange or amber (they're your
warnings, and close to Anthropic), plain blue (anonymous), and plain green
(hides your ticks).


==========================================================================
11. FROM SKETCH TO APP
==========================================================================

  1. Pick two or three directions from this file.
  2. Rough sketches on paper: each as a 16-point glyph first, then an icon.
  3. Run the six checks in part 9. Keep one.
  4. Draw the glyph's four states as vector shapes, one colour.
  5. Draw the app icon at 1024 points; check it at 32.
  6. Choose the colour's light and dark versions; check contrast.
  7. Into the app:
       - the glyph replaces the four stock symbols in Classic mode
       - the ring picks up the glyph's shape
       - the accent colour is set in one place
       - the app icon goes into the app bundle (a small change to how the
         app is built)
  8. With the icon in place, turn on the Dock icon while a window is open
     (item 63). Not before: without an icon the Dock shows a blank
     placeholder.
  9. Screenshots for the README, taken in demo mode.
